How this quiz works

Investor guidance from the SEC (Investor.gov) describes risk tolerance as both your ability and your willingness to lose money. This quiz scores those as two separate things:

  • Part A – Risk willingness (risk tolerance): how comfortable you feel with ups and downs and possible losses.
  • Part B – Risk capacity: how much loss your finances can actually absorb without harming your plans (time horizon, savings cushion, income, debt, reliance on the money).

Each part is scored separately. Your overall profile is the lower of the two, which is the approach used in CFA Institute research on risk profiling (and in this course, lesson 2.11). Answer with one goal in mind, such as retirement savings, and pick the option that is most true for you today, not what you think is the “right” answer.

Sources and research behind this quiz

The questions are original and were written for this course. They are inspired by (not copied from) the research and guidance below.

  1. Grable, J. E., & Lytton, R. H. (1999). Financial risk tolerance revisited: The development of a risk assessment instrument. Financial Services Review, 8(3), 163–181. A 13-item scale covering investment risk, risk comfort and experience, and speculative risk. Journal page · DOI
  2. Kuzniak, S., Rabbani, A., Heo, W., Ruiz-Menjivar, J., & Grable, J. E. (2015). The Grable and Lytton risk-tolerance scale: A 15-year retrospective. Financial Services Review. Reliability stayed robust (α ≈ 0.77, n = 160,279). Journal page

  4. U.S. SEC, Investor.gov, Asset Allocation and Diversification (defines risk tolerance as your ability and willingness to lose some or all of your investment, and time horizon). investor.gov
  5. UK FSA/FCA, FG11/05 Assessing suitability: Establishing the risk a customer is willing and able to take (2011), including “capacity for loss”. PDF
  6. ESMA (2022/2023), Guidelines on certain aspects of the MiFID II suitability requirements (ESMA35-43-3172), including ability to bear losses. PDF
  7. Pompian, M. M. (2016). Risk Profiling through a Behavioral Finance Lens. CFA Institute Research Foundation. cfainstitute.org
